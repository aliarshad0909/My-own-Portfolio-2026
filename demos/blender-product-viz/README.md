# Aster desk lamp — Blender product visualization

A complete, parametric Blender scene for a product shoot: model, materials,
three-point lighting, seamless backdrop, six camera angles and a turntable,
built by script so the whole setup is reproducible and version-controlled.

Project 08 of the portfolio expansion.

> **No renders exist yet.** Blender is not available in the environment where
> this was written. See `RENDER_STATUS.md` for exactly what is and is not
> verified. Nothing has been generated by other means and presented as a
> render.

## Why a script rather than a .blend file

A `.blend` is a binary you cannot diff, review or regenerate. This scene is
Python, so:

- Every lighting energy, camera angle and material value is readable in a text diff.
- A colourway or backdrop is a command-line flag, not a manual re-setup.
- Re-shooting a revised product means editing dimensions in one place.
- The camera maths is unit tested without opening Blender at all.

```bash
blender --background --python scenes/build_scene.py -- --preset studio_charcoal --finish sage --render-stills
```

## What the script builds

- **Product** — weighted base, slim stem, ball joint, angled head with a solidify modifier for wall thickness, and an emissive opal diffuser. Dimensions follow `specs/product-spec.md`.
- **Materials** — powder-coated aluminium, brushed stainless and a warm emissive diffuser, in three colourways.
- **Backdrop** — a curved sweep so there is no horizon line.
- **Lighting** — key, fill and rim area lights plus a low world ambient, with energies that change per backdrop preset. A dark backdrop needs a stronger rim and less ambient than a white one.
- **Cameras** — six, placed by spherical coordinates around a tracked empty, each with its own focal length and aperture. Detail shots are closer and longer than the hero.
- **Turntable** — 120 frames, rotating the product rather than the camera so the lighting stays put. 120 divides evenly into 24, 30 and 60fps, so it loops cleanly at any of them.

## Tests

```bash
python3 -m unittest discover -s tests -v    # 20 tests
```

They cover what can be checked without Blender:

| Group | Covers |
| --- | --- |
| Camera maths | Front, side and elevated placement; distance preserved from the target at every angle; negative elevation drops below the subject. |
| Angle table | Six distinct named angles, physically sensible distances and focal lengths, detail shots closer and longer than the hero, and no camera placed inside the product. |
| Presets | Every preset defines every key, valid RGBA backdrops, the key light is always strongest, and darker backdrops carry less ambient. |
| Arguments | Defaults, overrides, and rejection of an unknown preset. |
| Turntable | The frame count divides a full rotation evenly and suits 24, 30 and 60fps. |
| Module contract | Imports cleanly without Blender; every documented entry point exists. |

What they cannot cover: that `bpy` operators succeed, that the proportions look
right, or that the lighting flatters the product. Only Blender can show that.

## Layout

```
scenes/build_scene.py   The scene builder
specs/product-spec.md   Dimensions, materials, colourways, shot list, lighting
tests/test_scene.py     Tests for the Blender-free parts
renders/                Empty until someone renders it
RENDER_STATUS.md        What is verified and what is not
```
